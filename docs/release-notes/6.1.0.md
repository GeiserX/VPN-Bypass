## Before you upgrade

Nothing changes unless you turn something on. Your settings, lists and services carry over. The helper updates itself to 2.9.0 on the first launch. From helper 2.8.0, which 6.0.9 and later installed, that needs no password and no prompt appears, unless the helper's launchd file was changed or cannot be read. From an older helper, in that case, or when the self-update fails, macOS asks for the administrator password once.

## What's new

- **Per-app routing, off by default.** Until now VPN Bypass routed destinations, so a rule for an app did nothing. Turn on Settings > General > Per-app routing (or `vpnb engine.on`), and the apps you list reach the internet around the VPN whatever address they connect to: in Bypass mode from the new Apps list, in Custom mode from a rule for an app with the Direct route. It takes their TCP connections to public IPv4 addresses and nothing else. Other apps, IPv6, UDP, local addresses and the VPN client's own traffic keep their route, and domains, services and IP ranges keep working through the routes for every app. https://geiserx.github.io/VPN-Bypass/how-it-works/#per-app-routing
- **macOS asks twice when you turn it on.** First allow the extension in System Settings > General > Login Items & Extensions > Network Extensions, with your administrator password (the Open System Settings button under the switch in Settings > General opens that list; the one in macOS's alert does nothing on macOS 26); then allow VPN Bypass to add a proxy configuration. Say no and nothing else changes. VPN Bypass must be in the Applications folder. A Mac managed by an administrator may not allow it; Settings > General then says it is blocked.
- **It works past VPNs that use two half routes.** WireGuard (`wg-quick`), OpenVPN with `redirect-gateway def1` and similar VPNs take the internet with `0.0.0.0/1` and `128.0.0.0/1` and leave Wi-Fi or Ethernet without the route a listed app's connection needs. While per-app routing is on and the VPN is up, the helper adds that one route (a default route scoped to the physical interface), so the listed app gets through on its first connection, and removes it when per-app routing or the VPN goes off. It never changes the VPN's routes or the Mac's default route. https://geiserx.github.io/VPN-Bypass/how-it-works/#per-app-routing
- **When something goes wrong, the listed apps use the VPN.** If VPN Bypass quits, within 2 minutes. If the extension crashes, new connections wait about 5 seconds once, the app starts it again, and a second crash within 10 minutes turns it off.
- **Turning it off is the old way.** The switch removes the extension and its configuration (macOS asks for your administrator password), and the app works as 6.0 did. Turn it off before `brew uninstall`: Homebrew cannot remove a system extension. https://geiserx.github.io/VPN-Bypass/troubleshooting/#remove-the-per-app-routing-extension
- **`vpnb engine.status`, `engine.on`, `engine.off`, `engine.remove`** and **`vpnb app.list`, `app.add`, `app.rm`, `app.enable`, `app.disable`** do the same from a script. https://geiserx.github.io/VPN-Bypass/usage/#command-line-control-vpnb
